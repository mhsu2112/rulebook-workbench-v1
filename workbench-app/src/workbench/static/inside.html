⚖The Rulebook Workbench

A shared evidence base for transaction and post-trade reporting harmonisation.

The Taskforce is asked to find harmonisation, de-duplication and streamlining opportunities across UK MiFIR, UK EMIR and UK SFTR. Doing that well needs a citable account of where the three regimes actually diverge — and a way to compare the orders in which they could be tackled. This is a working prototype that produces exactly that.

01 — The problem the Taskforce faces first

The difficulty is not a shortage of ideas.

Up to fifty experienced people, meeting roughly every two months over an eighteen-month term, will generate more candidate harmonisations than the Taskforce can evaluate. Most will arrive as assertions — one regime defines a term differently from another, this field is collected twice, that requirement duplicates another — and there is no shared apparatus for establishing which assertions are correct, how much reporting volume each touches, and whether fixing any given one would change what firms are actually required to do.

That last question is the dividing line that determines what the Authorities can act on quickly. A divergence resolvable without altering any firm's obligations is available to the FCA and the Bank through their own technical standards and guidance. A divergence whose resolution would remove or alter a duty is a policy decision requiring consultation, and possibly statutory change. Both are worth finding. They are not the same kind of finding, and an output that mixes them is harder for officials to act on than one that separates them.

“As defined above, please rank in order of importance: De-duplication / Streamlining / Harmonisation.”

Strategy Working Group — preparation work, Part 1

The three levers are not independent. A firm cannot stop filing a duplicate report until two regimes agree what counts as the same trade; a field cannot safely be deleted until someone establishes which regime or source holds that data authoritatively. Work done in one order may have to be redone in another. The order has not been decided, and there are reasonable arguments for more than one. That decision deserves evidence rather than instinct.

What this looks like if it works. A single register the Taskforce and the Authorities both work from, in which every claimed divergence between the three regimes is a numbered entry with the text that establishes it, the volume of reporting it touches, and a stated position on whether resolving it changes any firm's obligations — so that argument moves from whether a problem exists to what should be done about it.
What already exists — and the gap none of it fills
CP25/32
Already reshaping the MiFIR field set. Any harmonisation analysis must take the post-consultation position as its reference point, not the current one.
UK EMIR Refit
Recently implemented, so the EMIR field set and validation rules are comparatively settled and well documented.
Published validation rules
The Bank's and the FCA's rules and schemas are already substantially machine-readable — much of the raw material this needs has been produced by the Authorities for other reasons.
CDE and ISO 20022
International data standardisation supplies definitions the UK regimes can align to without either regime having to defer to the other.
Industry modelling
Cross-regime concept modelling is technically mature. That is evidence of feasibility — not an argument that the Authorities should adopt any particular industry model.
The gap
A systematic, citable account of where the three regimes diverge from one another, held in one place, in a form that supports comparison of alternative reform sequences.

03 — The method

A fixed sequence. Each step leaves a document you can open and read.

A corpus is assembled and frozen; each instrument is read and its obligations, definitions and cross-references extracted with verbatim citations; the extractions are compared to surface divergences; each divergence is worked into one or more proposed changes to the analytical picture; and each proposal is classified, reviewed and either adopted or set aside by a named person.

A short structured interview pins down the scope, the decision the result must support, and the mode election: refactor (describe and tidy the analytical picture without altering any obligation) or redesign (reshape it against objectives someone with authority has adopted).

The mode is declared up front and frozen. In refactor mode a proposal that would alter a duty cannot be completed, however sensible it looks. That single election is what makes everything after it auditable.

Produces → Purpose Statement — typed, schema-enforced, ratified by a named person with a written rationale

Source discovery runs in the panel along the bottom: the system proposes candidate instruments, a person confirms or sets each aside with a logged reason, and the resulting list is frozen with a fingerprint. The analysis cannot then shift quietly onto different sources.

All material here is published. Where firms later contribute cost or volume data, the system already separates governed from restricted storage.

Produces → Frozen manifest — hashed, dated, with every set-aside recorded

Every duty (who must do what, when, above what threshold, with what exceptions), every defined term, and every cross-reference, each carrying a verbatim quotation checked mechanically against the source. A quotation that does not appear in the source text is rejected by the system rather than reviewed by a human.

Faithful means faithful. Where the instruments contradict one another or define a term two ways, the blueprint records the divergence rather than resolving it. Resolution is a later step, taken by a person, on the record.

Produces → Derived Blueprint + Divergence Register — the numbered findings, each with the text that establishes it

Proposals are drawn from a fixed whitelist of named moves — merge, canonicalise a definition, define a term, resolve a cross-reference, relocate, repeal, factor out an exception. There is no free-form rewrite, so every proposal has a type and every type has rules.

Each arrives with a drafted effect class and a confidence, written by a different model from the one that extracted the obligation. That draft is advice with no weight of its own; the reviewer's classification is the record, and it is retained alongside for audit.

Produces → Dispositioned register + register of policy questions requiring consultation

A person or body with actual authority first adopts and ranks a short list of objectives, stated as outcomes rather than instruments. Every proposed change must then name the objective it serves; one with no objective behind it cannot enter review at all — it is returned, not rejected.

Where a move advances one objective at another's cost and the ranking is silent, nobody in the queue may resolve it. It goes back up as a proposed amendment. The workbench surfaces trade-offs; it does not price them.

Produces → Ratified objectives · Misalignment Register · dispositioned changes, each signed

The Derived Blueprint plus every adopted proposal, each still carrying who adopted it and why, and an explicit list of what was set aside. That list is not a failure; it is the honest record of what cannot be settled without a policy decision somebody has to own.

Produces → Target Blueprint — an advisory description of a possible target state, addressed to the Authorities

A crosswalk: every source provision on the left, its treatment on the right — merged, retained, or set aside — each with a reason. Above it sits the effect-class audit, the mechanical confirmation of the human claim that nothing in the exercise altered an obligation.

Produces → Crosswalk + effect-class audit

Model assignment per task is itself a decision that must be taken and locked before the corpus can be frozen. What actually ran, at what cost, appears in the Record panel — so the configuration and the execution can be compared after the fact.

Produces → Locked model assignment — logged, stamped, and diversity-checked at call time

Eighteen instruments, frozen and read. Seventy-one findings.

05 — Three findings, walked through

The interesting part is not what the model proposed. It is what the reviewer did with it.

One straightforward adoption, one where the governance held under pressure, and one the system refused to complete.

Finding F-038 — the straightforward adoption
MERGEdraft: clarify (high)duplicated requirement

The duty to notify errors and omissions is separately stated in the MiFIR, EMIR and SFTR guidance. Record it once in the blueprint, with the three statements as its sources.

targets 3  ·  quotes 3/3 ✓ verified against the frozen corpus
Details — the model's reasoning

Three separate guidance statements impose the same duty on the same population, in materially the same terms. They were compared element by element — who is bound, what must be notified, to whom, and within what period — rather than by phrasing.

Finding F-041 — where the governance held
CANONICALIZE-DEFINITIONdraft: clarify (medium)divergent definition

Adopt a single definition of financial counterparty across the UK EMIR onshoring instrument and UK SFTR.

targets 2  ·  quotes 4/4 ✓ verified
Details — and why the reviewer overrode the draft

The proposal is attractive on its face: one term, two regimes, two definitions, and harmonisation is the Taskforce's first-named objective. The quotations are genuine and verified.

But the two regimes enumerate different lists of entities. Unifying the definition would not describe the position more clearly — it would move entities into or out of scope, and therefore change who has to report. That is a policy decision requiring consultation, not a description.

The system reached this outcome without anyone having to catch the error: the reviewer's classification routed the finding automatically, and the refactor-mode gate would have refused an adoption had one been attempted.

06 — The separation that matters to officials

Two registers, not one list.

Every finding leaves the review classified into one of two places. The classification is a named person's, with a written rationale, and the software refuses to complete an operation without one.

Available to the Authorities directly

Resolvable without altering any firm's obligations

Actionable through the FCA's and the Bank's own technical standards and guidance. Typically:

  • the same duty separately stated in each regime's guidance, recorded once with three sources
  • a cross-reference pointing at a provision that no longer exists
  • a term used consistently in substance but described in three different ways
  • obsolete provisions with no remaining operative effect
Requires consultation

Resolution would remove or alter a duty

A policy decision, possibly needing statutory change. Recorded as a numbered question with its evidence attached. Typically:

  • definitions whose unification would move entities into or out of scope
  • conflicting deadlines where picking a winner changes what a firm must do
  • single-sided versus dual-sided reporting where the regimes differ by design
  • findings the exercise records as beyond what it can properly resolve
Why the split is the point. Both kinds of finding are worth having. They are not the same kind of finding, and an output that mixes them is harder for officials to act on than one that separates them. The first register is a work programme the Authorities already have the competence to execute. The second is an agenda for consultation, arriving with its evidence rather than needing it assembled later.
07 — Comparing sequences, rather than assuming one

The Taskforce has not chosen an order. The tool should not choose one either.

Once divergences are recorded at the level of individual data elements, the dependencies between them can be computed rather than debated. If a proposal to stop collecting a field twice depends on two regimes first agreeing what the field means, the register knows it. The same set of findings can then be run against each candidate ordering and what each one costs reported on five measures.

MeasureThe question it answers
ReworkHow much work would have to be done twice, because a later phase changes a definition an earlier phase relied on?
Early coverageWhat share of reporting volume is addressed before the first decision point?
Blocked findingsHow many items cannot properly be worked in the phase they are assigned to?
When policy questions surfaceDo the questions requiring consultation emerge early enough to be acted on within the Taskforce's term?
Dependence on statutory changeHow much of an ordering's value requires change beyond the FCA's and the Bank's own competence, and is therefore slower in practice?
The tool ranks; it does not choose. The output is a comparison with its assumptions stated, for the Taskforce to weigh. Where the Taskforce elects an order that works items ahead of their dependencies, the system does not refuse — it records which items are affected and requires the choice to be acknowledged in writing. Where no order is elected, whatever default the software applies is labelled in every output as a default the Taskforce did not choose, so that silence never resembles a decision.

On the estimates of volume and burden that make the comparison possible: these begin as one member's cross-firm estimates and are labelled as such throughout, with every figure carrying its basis and confidence. The register is designed for firms to overwrite them with their own cost accounting. That path — from one person's estimate to the Taskforce's evidence base — is the point of holding the figures in a structured register at all.

08 — What the run does not yet see

The more useful result is where the current approach falls short.

The seventy-one findings concern the legal text of the three regimes: definitions, cross-references, obsolete provisions, duplicated duties. They contain almost nothing about the data the regimes collect.

48% / 45%
Valuation updates as a share of transaction-level activity under EMIR and SFTR respectively, on the Taskforce's own figures — and the run says nothing about them
38%
Position components as a share of EMIR transaction-level volumes — likewise absent from the findings
Static data
Counterparty identifiers, entity attributes and instrument data restated on every message though published authoritatively elsewhere — not visible to the analysis
ETDs, twice
The same instrument reported to the FCA under MiFIR and to a Trade Repository under EMIR, on different event models — not visible either
The reason is structural. The analysis models instruments and provisions, not data elements — so it cannot see element-level duplication, which is where most of the reporting burden sits. Closing that gap is the substance of what would need to be built.

This is stated plainly because a tool that only reports its successes is not much use as an evidence base. The legal-text layer was the right place to start — it is where the method's guarantees are easiest to verify — but it is not where the Taskforce's problem lives.

09 — Four properties, and four refusals

The trustworthy part of a tool like this is the list of things it will not do.

Everything is cited, and citations are checked mechanically

Every finding and every proposal carries verbatim quotations from the source instruments. Quotations that do not appear in the source text are rejected by the system rather than reviewed by a human. A finding that cannot be evidenced does not enter the register.

Every judgement is a person's

Language models read, compare and draft. They classify nothing. Whether a proposed change would alter a firm's obligations is determined by a named human reviewer with a written rationale; the model's suggestion is retained alongside for audit and carries no weight on its own.

Changes to obligations cannot be made by accident

Where a reviewer classifies a proposal as altering what firms must do, the system refuses to complete it in a harmonisation exercise and sets it aside into the register of policy questions. This is enforced in the software, not by convention.

Every decision is logged, permanently

The corpus is content-hashed and frozen before analysis begins; each decision is written to an append-only log with the decider, their role, the time and the reasoning. The output can be audited long after the fact by someone who was not present.

Does not

Draft rules or produce operative legal text. Its outputs are advisory descriptions of a possible target state, addressed to an authority.

Does not

Express regulatory requirements as executable code. This is not a policy-as-code proposal; the statutory instruments, technical standards and Handbook text remain the sole authoritative sources throughout.

Does not

Decide anything, or hold a view on what the Taskforce should recommend. Where its analysis and the instruments differ, the instruments govern, without qualification.

Does not

Require the Authorities to adopt any external standard, technology or vendor. Where an international standard supplies a neutral definition, the register records it as available. It mandates nothing.

The questions it should expect
ObjectionResponse
“Is an AI making regulatory judgements?”No. Models read, compare and draft; they classify nothing and decide nothing. Every determination of whether a change would alter firms' obligations is made by a named person with a written rationale, and the software refuses to complete an operation without one. Quotations are verified against source text mechanically, so a fabricated citation cannot enter the register.
“Who owns the interpretation?”The Authorities, entirely. The tool produces an analytical description of where instruments diverge. It is not a source of interpretation and confers no authority.
“Does this presuppose an answer on sequencing?”It is designed not to. Any default ordering is labelled as a default, alternative orderings are compared on the same evidence, and the comparison is offered for the Taskforce to weigh rather than as a recommendation.
“Does it duplicate CP25/32 or the Refit?”No. Those set what the requirements are; this describes where the three regimes diverge from one another once they are set. The consultation outcome would be the reference point for the analysis, not a competitor to it.
“Are we being asked to adopt a standard, a vendor or a technology?”No. It recommends nothing, mandates nothing, and takes no position on how firms build.
“What about confidentiality?”The corpus is published material. The system already separates governed from restricted storage; any firm-supplied figures would be held under whatever confidentiality terms the Taskforce sets, and the analytical registers would carry only what firms agree to publish.
“What does it cost?”The computational cost is negligible — roughly a quarter of a dollar per finding analysed, so the entire existing run cost a few tens of dollars. The real cost is expert human time reviewing findings and classifying effects, which cannot be automated without destroying the value.
“What if the estimates are wrong?”They probably are, in detail. They are labelled as estimates with their basis stated, the comparison is reported both with and without them, and the register is built for firms to replace them with real figures. The shape of the distribution matters more than any individual number, and the shape is testable.
10 — What is proposed

Nothing is proposed for adoption.

What follows is a description of what could be built and on what terms, offered so that colleagues can judge whether it is worth the Taskforce's attention — most naturally the Architecture Working Group's, given its remit on technology to streamline reporting.

Phase 1 · approximately five weeks

A worked example, for discussion

Take one concept family — how a trade's lifecycle events are represented, where EMIR uses a defined vocabulary of action types, SFTR uses another, and MiFIR has no clean equivalent — and model it exhaustively at field level across all three regimes. Produce the divergence register for that family, with citations, and a worked sequence comparison over it.

Success criterion: members with direct reporting experience agree the register describes real divergences accurately and that nothing material in that family is missing.

Off-ramp: if the register is wrong, incomplete, or tells members nothing they did not already know, the work stops here having cost five weeks and no institutional commitment.

Phase 2 · subject to Phase 1

Extension across the field sets

Extend the element-level model to the full field sets of the three regimes, drawing on the Authorities' published validation rules and schemas, and produce the complete divergence register, the register of policy questions requiring consultation, and the sequence comparison across all three levers.

Success criterion: the register is complete enough to serve as a shared reference for Taskforce discussion, and the separation between what the Authorities can act on directly and what needs consultation holds up to scrutiny.

Phase 3 · deliberately unspecified

Whatever the Authorities find useful

If the earlier phases produce something officials want to maintain rather than merely read, that is a decision for the Authorities to take with the benefit of evidence, not one to design for now.

Status: a working prototype built independently. Not a product, not offered for sale, and nothing about it built to a specification the Authorities have set.

Three things that would help most at this stage
From colleagues with direct reporting experience — whether the divergence register describes the problem as you encounter it, or misses its substance.
From officials — whether the separation between findings the Authorities could act on directly and findings requiring consultation is drawn where you would draw it.
On the worked example — whether one concept family would be useful, and if so which. Lifecycle events are the current proposal, but that is a judgement others are better placed to make.
