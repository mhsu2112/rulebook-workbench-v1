Target Blueprint — liquidity-redesign-p2

Mode: redesign · 98 finalized operations · effect classes: change 18, clarify 50, codify 26, fill_gap 4 · rendered 2026-07-21 02:48 UTC
Ratified scope. Produce a comprehensive diagnostic of the current U.S. bank liquidity regime as it pertains to fast-moving bank runs — mapping the fragmentation and lack of interoperability between Federal Reserve discount-window and FHLB collateral/operational rules, cataloguing the structural sources of discount-window stigma, and identifying where the timing and granularity of liquidity reporting (Call Reports, FR 2052a, and related instruments) itself contributes to slow detection or response — together with a defect register and a mapping of reform proposals (including those from Acting Comptroller Hsu and Undersecretary McKernan) against the identified defects, as of today's date, to serve as the evidentiary foundation on which a future, separately chartered, Principal-led redesign program can be convened. [Program 2: redesign under the Ratified Mandate, baseline sha256:214707d2212e86ce239c2bdc8005fc0824e09c8a6f5338d3f8c279e436098d08]
This is the Target Blueprint in operation-trace form: the Derived Blueprint (its element tables) plus the ordered, finalized operations below, each with the human disposition that approved it. A fully materialized composite — the element tables rewritten — is a later step (backlog B6). Provision-level accountability (where every legacy rule went) is in the Crosswalk (Align tab); the underlying element tables are in the Derived Blueprint.
Ratified Mandate (the authority for every change below) — ranking O1 > O2 > O3 > O4 > O5
O1: Maintain confidence in banks and the banking system under fast-run conditions
O2: Avoid the need for bailouts
O3: Improve banks' survival odds in rapid runs and reduce contagion risk (via revived Fed lender-of-last-resort functionality)
O4: Free up bank balance-sheet capacity for economic activity, currently tied up in liquidity self-insurance
O5: Limit moral hazard
